Privacy

Bunko · LazyingArt LLC · updated 30 September 2026

Reading remains local and does not require an account. Bunko has no analytics, advertising identifier, or crash reporting. Optional public discussions use GitHub. In versions with in-app posting, a small LazyingArt cloud service handles GitHub authorization and forwards the comments you choose to publish.

What stays on your device

Which books and optional offline dictionaries you downloaded, where you stopped reading in each book, your private passage notes, unpublished comment drafts, hidden-reader preferences, recently viewed dictionary definitions, and your settings: languages shown, arrangement, readings, text size and theme. All of it is written to your device's local storage by the app itself. Deleting the app deletes it. These local reading settings, passage notes and comment drafts are not synchronised. Documents you explicitly upload to the optional companion are separate, as described below.

What leaves your device, and where it goes

When you open the library or download a book or dictionary, the app fetches files from the public GitHub repository lachlanchen/bunko-books, through GitHub's own servers and the jsDelivr content network. A downloaded dictionary is searched entirely on your device. If you have not downloaded a dictionary, looking up a word requests its definition from Wiktionary. When you open a passage's public discussion, Bunko requests matching issues and comments from GitHub, directly or through its discussion service. Those services see ordinary web requests, including an IP address, a time, and the requested book file, dictionary pack, word, or discussion. Their handling is described by GitHub, jsDelivr, and Wikimedia. Book and dictionary downloads do not pass through the Bunko discussion service.

If you use “Request a book”, the app opens GitHub's issue form in your browser with the title you typed. Nothing is sent until you choose to submit it there, and what you submit is public.

If you use in-app GitHub sign-in, GitHub handles your password and consent in its secure browser. Bunko's cloud service receives your public GitHub user ID and username and an access token limited to Bunko's discussion repository. From version 1.0.6, access and refresh tokens remain encrypted on the server, and GitHub access tokens refresh automatically. An opaque Bunko session is stored in iOS/macOS Keychain or Android Keystore-protected storage; the web reader uses a Secure, HttpOnly cookie. Sessions remain active during normal use and expire after 90 days without authenticated use, or sooner if GitHub authorization expires or is revoked. Signing out deletes the server session and clears local sign-in storage; an offline sign-out clears native credentials immediately and the web cookie is cleared when the service is reachable. iOS Keychain entries can survive reinstalling the app. Version 1.0.5 keeps only an in-memory session for up to eight hours. You can revoke Bunko's authorization at any time in GitHub's application settings.

The service processes your IP address for short-lived rate limits, passage IDs to find conversations, and the excerpt and comment only when you press Post. Authorization attempts expire after ten minutes. Expired sessions are deleted automatically. A record of a submitted public comment may be kept for seven days to prevent duplicate posts; passage-to-issue mappings remain to locate conversations. Discussion responses may be cached briefly. Application logs exclude request bodies, credentials, and OAuth callback queries.

Your submitted post is public under your GitHub account, or clearly labelled as a Bunko demo-account post when you use the invited demo account. You can edit or delete your own posts on GitHub, report content there, or hide a reader locally in Bunko. Signing out does not delete public posts. Repository maintainers moderate discussions under GitHub's rules. Private notes and unpublished drafts never become public unless you explicitly submit them as a comment.

Private document companion (version 1.0.7 and later)

When you choose a file or import a paper, Bunko uploads that document to its cloud service under your Bunko sign-in identity. PDF, Word, Markdown, text and TeX uploads are optional. Files, converted text and figures, and companion conversations are private; they are not published to GitHub or shared with other readers. Documents and conversations are encrypted at rest and sent over HTTPS.

PDF conversion sends the file to Mathpix to extract text, equations and figures. Paper searches send your search terms to research indexes such as arXiv, OpenAlex and Europe PMC. Companion replies send your question, recent conversation and relevant document passages to DeepSeek. Do not upload material you are not permitted to process with these services. The companion can make mistakes; compare its answers with the original paper.

Temporary originals are removed from Bunko's service after successful conversion. Failed or interrupted uploads remain available for retry until you remove them. Converted documents and conversations remain until you remove the document or choose Clear private library. Clearing the library also removes reports you submitted about assistant responses. Usage counters are retained for up to 30 days to enforce service limits. Restricted operational backups expire within 30 days. Service providers apply their own retention policies. Signing out does not delete your private library; sign in again to access or remove it.

Invited demo accounts (from 1.0.9). The clearly labelled Demo account sign-in accepts dedicated credentials supplied privately to testers or store reviewers; it never asks for the owner’s email verification code. The server stores a salted password hash and issues the same protected Bunko sessions, with a 90-day inactivity limit and sign-out. Demo documents and companion conversations belong to the demo identity, separate from ordinary readers, and are shared by people using that demo account. Do not upload sensitive files to it. Comments are real public GitHub posts labelled “Bunko demo account” and sent through a GitHub App limited to the Bunko book repository. Demo testers can delete their companion data in the app and request moderation or deletion of public demo posts using the contact below. Changing or disabling the demo credentials invalidates its old sessions.

Disconnect Bunko and request deletion

Bunko uses your existing GitHub account for ordinary sign-in. Invited testers and store reviewers can also use a separately provisioned demo account; Bunko does not offer public password-account registration. To remove your Bunko sign-in, open a passage discussion and choose Sign out while online. This deletes that server session, including its stored GitHub user ID, username and encrypted authorization tokens, and clears the sign-in on that device. Sign out on each device you use. You can also revoke Bunko Reading Conversations in GitHub application settings.

To request deletion of all Bunko service sessions, private documents, conversations or associated data, email lachlan.mia.chan@gmail.com with the subject “Bunko data deletion” and your public GitHub username. You can request this without deleting your GitHub account. We may ask you to verify control of that account; never send a password or token.

Public comments remain on GitHub until you delete them there or request help from the repository maintainers. Signing out does not delete those posts or your GitHub account. Duplicate-post records expire after seven days, inactive sessions after 90 days, and anonymous passage-to-issue links may remain to keep public conversations accessible. Private notes, reading history and drafts remain on your device; removing Bunko's local app data deletes those local records.

Children

Bunko is a reading app for works of literature. Public passage discussions may contain user-generated text hosted by GitHub. Ordinary GitHub sign-in follows GitHub’s account rules. Invited demo accounts post through Bunko’s repository-scoped GitHub App with explicit demo attribution. Bunko has no advertising and no purchases inside it.
